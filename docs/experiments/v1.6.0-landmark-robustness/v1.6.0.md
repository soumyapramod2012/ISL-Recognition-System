# v1.6.0 Landmark Robustness Experiment

## 1. Experiment Overview

Version: v1.6.0

Experiment:
Landmark Robustness Analysis

Parent Model:
v1.5.0

Model Status:
Frozen v1.5.0 model

Model File:
saved_models/isl_lstm.keras

Purpose:
Evaluate whether hand landmark interpolation improves model robustness when landmark observations are artificially removed from the test sequences.

The experiment does not retrain the model. The frozen v1.5.0 model is used throughout the experiment.

---

## 2. Test Dataset

Test samples: 17

Classes: 6

Feature shape:

(17, 60, 258)

Clean baseline accuracy:

17/17 = 1.0000

The clean baseline confirms that the frozen v1.5.0 model correctly classifies all 17 test samples before artificial landmark corruption is introduced.

---

## 3. Experiment Design

Artificial landmark gaps were introduced into the test sequences.

Tested gap lengths:

- 2 frames
- 5 frames
- 10 frames
- 20 frames

For each corrupted sequence, predictions were evaluated under two conditions:

1. Without interpolation
2. With hand landmark interpolation

The same frozen v1.5.0 model was used for both conditions.

## 7. Interpolation Strategy

The experiment uses the HandLandmarkInterpolator.

The interpolation strategy operates independently on the left and right hand landmark sequences.

A missing hand frame is identified when all hand landmark coordinates for that frame are zero.

Only short gaps are interpolated.

The interpolation requires:

- A valid frame immediately before the gap
- A valid frame immediately after the gap
- Gap length not exceeding the configured maximum gap

The interpolation uses linear interpolation between the surrounding valid landmark frames.

The v1.5.0 interpolation configuration uses:

max_gap = 5

Therefore, gaps longer than the configured interpolation limit are not repaired by the interpolation mechanism.

---

## 8. Artificial Landmark Analysis

The robustness experiment additionally tracks artificially introduced missing landmarks and the number of artificial landmarks subsequently repaired.

This allows the experiment to distinguish between naturally missing landmarks and missing landmarks introduced specifically for the robustness experiment.

For gap lengths of 2 and 5 frames, some of the artificially introduced missing landmarks were successfully repaired by the interpolation mechanism.

For gap lengths of 10 and 20 frames, the artificially introduced gaps exceeded the configured interpolation limit of 5 frames. Consequently, the artificial landmarks were not repaired by the interpolator in these trials.

The experiment therefore provides evidence that the current interpolation strategy is specifically effective for short artificial gaps within its configured maximum gap length.

---

## 9. Interpretation

The experiment demonstrates that short-gap hand landmark interpolation improves robustness to artificially introduced landmark gaps.

The strongest average improvement was observed for a gap length of 5 frames:

+0.0706 mean accuracy improvement.

The improvement decreases as the artificial gap becomes longer.

At gap length 20, interpolation still provides a positive mean improvement of +0.0294, but the result is less stable.

At gap length 20:

- 7 trials improved
- 1 trial was unchanged
- 2 trials became worse

Therefore, interpolation should not be interpreted as universally beneficial for arbitrarily long landmark gaps.

---

## 10. Key Findings

1. The frozen v1.5.0 model achieved 100% accuracy on the clean 17-sample test set.

2. Artificial landmark gaps reduce classification accuracy.

3. Hand landmark interpolation consistently improves performance for short gaps.

4. Gap length 5 produced the strongest mean improvement across the tested conditions.

5. The benefit decreases for longer artificial gaps.

6. At gap length 20, interpolation can occasionally reduce accuracy.

7. The current interpolation strategy is therefore most strongly supported for short landmark gaps.

---

## 11. Limitations

The robustness experiment uses a 17-sample test set.

The landmark gaps are artificially introduced rather than collected from naturally corrupted videos.

Therefore, the results demonstrate robustness to simulated landmark loss rather than establishing performance under all real-world landmark detection failures.

The experiment should be treated as a controlled robustness study.

---

## 12. Conclusion

v1.6.0 provides evidence that short-gap hand landmark interpolation can improve the robustness of the frozen v1.5.0 Indian Sign Language recognition model.

The multi-trial results show consistent gains for gap lengths of 2, 5, and 10 frames, while the benefit becomes less reliable at 20 frames.

The results support continued investigation of landmark-handling strategies in v1.7.0, particularly strategies that can distinguish between short recoverable landmark gaps and longer or unreliable missing-landmark regions.
